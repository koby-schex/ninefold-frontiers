# World Five — The Veyrath (WORKING NAME)
## Faction & initiation design pass 01
**Status:** proposed faction name, origin, mechanics, hierarchy and roster. **User-directed premise:** the principal World Five campaign enemies are splinter Avarin with a markedly different culture and appearance; joining requires personally killing one's *existing, willing bonded partner* in a disturbing rite that grants real power at a real cost. The killing is betrayal, not consensual partnership. Do not treat this draft as a canon archive release before explicit review.

### Defining identity
**Working faction name:** **Veyrath** (pronounced VAY-rath). Outsiders call them the **Unbound**; members call themselves **the Kept** — believing those who can stand after the rite have been kept by World Five itself. This is a proposed local Avarin tradition and splinter network, not a separate species or the whole Fractured Rim population.

**Creed:** “What we loved could not carry us. What we surrendered will.” Founding survivors claim the Continuance's route promises failed in a catastrophic, long-ago evacuation near the Lost Marches. Their leaders argue that dependence on companions leaves Avarin unable to survive when routes separate them. They interpret a cruel choice as ultimate independence, although the rite creates its own dependency.

**History (proposal):** a failed corridor collapse scattered a mixed Continuance rescue convoy. Several survivors deliberately turned fragments of broken wayfinding equipment into unstable Arcana concentrators. One surviving bond partner was killed during an improvised activation that let a handful cross. Later leaders converted this horror into a compulsory initiation and distorted the history to present it as necessary. Exact date, responsible people and relation to any protected main-game mystery remain undecided, and must NOT be retroactively established here.

### The rite — The Parting (working name)
Candidates must already have a real, voluntary partnership with a native creature. The cult's initiation deliberately violates the trust that made that partnership possible: during a communal rite, the candidate **kills their own bonded creature** while a scavenged fracture-tuned anchor vessel captures the brief Arcana-rich environmental disturbance caused by the violent interruption of the partner's body, equipment and surrounding field. The candidate then accepts an irreversible imprint from the vessel. No gore, detailed ritual procedure, or celebratory presentation is needed; portray the horror through the trust betrayed, the absence afterward, and the survivors' reactions.

**Mechanism boundaries:** The power comes from unstable Arcana capture and reconfiguration at the ritual site, NOT from the creature's soul, stolen loyalty, psychic leash, or mantle as a bonding organ. The Avarin mantle *continues to sense environmental/Arcana changes* as canon already states, but a new uncontrolled imprint amplifies its reaction and distorts interpretation. The process cannot be cleanly mass-produced or repeatedly farmed for power. A candidate does not gain the specific creature partner's skills or physiology; species of former partner is not a deterministic class selector.

**Gain — Severance Surge:** brief periods of supernormal fracture perception, high-risk short displacement, disruptive pressure bursts, and unusually forceful movement across unstable edges. The cult mistakes the raw intensity of their sensing for superior mastery, even though its accuracy worsens when stressed. Specific combat values are **not** locked.

**Permanent cost — The Afterecho:** recurring false route-sensations, overload near anchors, intrusive phantom impressions of movement beside the initiate, increasing inability to tolerate stable sanctuary fields, and impaired judgment during prolonged engagement. Sudden intensity can help them win a skirmish but leaves them disoriented and vulnerable afterward. Crucially, this is an unpredictable environmental/neurological cost, **not** cosmic moral punishment. Some initiates hide the symptoms, others eventually leave; leaving does not resurrect the creature or erase the harm.

**What the cult conceals:** The vessel can be activated by fracture instability without killing a beloved companion, though much less efficiently and unpredictably; leaders suppress nonlethal experimentation because the rite's violence is the real loyalty test. This is a proposed internal contradiction, not yet a story twist to publish.

### Visual identity
Avarin **remain recognizably Avarin**: same overall bipedal anatomy, layered temple-face, forehead seam, and broad crescent environmental-sensory mantle. Do not remove, replace with horns, or reinvent the mantle as a bond conduit. Cultural modifications distinguish them:
- scorched/scoured dark mineral clasps mounted in asymmetrical mantles **without cutting off the living mantle**;
- layered ash-black route wraps, salvaged fracture plates and fiber lashings over warm charcoal/plum-gray tissue;
- pale fracture-glass splinters embedded in **equipment**, not mandatory bodily mutilation;
- irregular crescent-profile cloaks and mask-like lower-face coverings that do not change actual species anatomy;
- flickering discontinuous violet-gray Arcana readout from hardware near the mantle, distinct from healthy localized mantle bioluminescence;
- deliberately empty harness attachment points or carried unoccupied paired harness rings: an unmistakable memorial/accusation of an absent companion, not trophies of a slain species.
Hierarchy is expressed through scarred equipment, route relics and discipline, **not** simple red-black “evil armor.” Social life includes survivors, doubters, caregivers and coercive leaders; not everyone born among them has undergone the rite.

### Society and internal conflict
Settlements are mobile kin-camps, cave shelters and scavenged crossing posts, not a world-spanning empire. They trade, raise children, shelter their own, and conduct pragmatic scavenging; leaders selectively control exits from their camps. Those who refuse the rite can remain marginal laborers, escape, or face expulsion depending on camp. Converts are judged on the act of severance; the campaign can expose reluctant initiates and defectors. Individual responsibility remains possible; the Continuance's answer is rescue and accountability rather than judging every Avarin outside its customs.

### First game enemy families — **working gameplay roles, not universal classes**
1. **Rift Forager** — rapid, uninitiated route scavenger, ordinary tools, may flee; no Severance Surge.
2. **Parted Striker** — initiated close-range skirmisher: burst reposition then accuracy/defense penalty after surge.
3. **Seam Castigator** — initiated area-denial tactician: short disruptive pressure wave with visible buildup and Afterecho recovery.
4. **Anchor Wrecker** — equipment-focused sabotage specialist: destabilizes terrain or mission objectives, but is vulnerable while setting a device.
5. **Afterecho Seer** — heavily affected veteran who can sense movement unpredictably; dangerous telegraphed disruption and periods of self-disorientation.
6. **Camp Witness** — leader/organizer who coordinates allies and manipulates fear; no assumption that all leaders are strongest warriors.
Avoid binding these to the optional universal Frontline/Damage/Support UI descriptors. No cultist may summon or command a bonded creature as their default ability.
